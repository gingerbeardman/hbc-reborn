# HBC 1.16.x: findings and hardware results

What each 1.16.x change found, fixed and measured, newest first. The short list of
changes is in [CHANGELOG.md](../../CHANGELOG.md); open items are in
[review.md](../review.md).

## 1.16.3: the network settles before HBC listens; a Wi-Fi that does not join gets a fresh IOS at once

The 1.16.2 log ruled out the probe: the early `ENETRESET` came without it.
Across all 375 network starts in `sd:/hbc-network.log` (2026-11-12), an
early reset (within 3 s) followed:

| Before this boot | Link 4 at up | Link 5 at up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| an app | 26 of 26 | 65 of 320 |
| HBC's own relaunch | 1 of 1 | 3 of 21 |
| a cold boot | 1 of 1 | 0 of 6 |

It came 0.7-1.27 s after `net_init` returned. A `net_deinit` and `net_init`
in the same IOS never recovered from it (-116 after about 13 s); only a fresh
IOS did.

- `loader.c`: HBC listens only once NCD's link has been up `NET_SETTLE_MS`
  (2.5 s) in a row from the start. A link at 4 (wireless down) or 1 (busy)
  in that time goes to the fresh-IOS relaunch ("the Wi-Fi did not finish
  joining"). Other values (errors, Dolphin) count as up.
- That relaunch has its own limit, `HBC_NET_JOIN_RELAUNCHES` (6) in a row
  (`netinit_reset_relaunch`, counted from the relaunches that led to this
  boot, which a start's success does not clear).
- A reset once listening: HBC's sockets closed, up to 5 s for the link, a
  fresh listener checked with one accept ("Relisten:"); if that fails, the
  same relaunch. Where HBC may not relaunch, Restart network as before.
- For 30 s after each start every change of NCD's link is logged ("Link:").
- `tests/wii/wii_launch_stress.py --back-s S`: how long HBC may take to come
  back after a launch (default 90 s), so a slow recovery is measured.

What the Wii showed on the way there, with `tests/wii/wii_launch_stress.py`
(`agent_app` launched and back, 20 rounds each) and slicks-test runs:

- **A Wi-Fi that does not join stays that way in that IOS.** The bad boots
  all went link 4 at about +60 ms, 5 for about 50 ms at about +1.95 s, then
  4 for the rest of a 15 s wait. A clean exit by the app (network up, its
  `net_deinit` done) did not prevent it. A fresh IOS fixed it, sometimes the
  second or third.
- **A fresh listener did not help** (link 4 and no address 5.5 s later,
  twice), so the relaunch is decided at the end of the hold.
- **A first form that relaunched at the first link 4 hit its limit of 3**
  in one run (three IOSes in a row still joining) and fell back to the slow
  path (about 100 s); hence the limit of 6.
- **An agent wait at exit for the network start made it worse**: 9 bad
  returns in 20 rounds with it, 1 in 20 without; dropped.

The final form, 20 rounds: 12 clean (about 18 s from launch to HBC
answering), 6 with one relaunch (about 31.5 s), 1 with two (45 s), and 1 where
HBC's own `net_init` stayed busy for its 20 s and the cleanup then hung 30 s
before the relaunch (79 s; open, unchanged). All 20 came back by themselves.
With the 15 s wait a return that did not join took about 70 s.

`tests/host/test_netinit.py` checks the relaunch limit across starts that
succeed.

## 1.16.2: the gateway probe waits for the link; libogc 3.2.1 breaks the Wii Remotes

After an app went back to HBC, the network often dropped and HBC relaunched
itself. `sd:/hbc-network.log` on Matt's Wii (21,604 lines, 2026-11-12) had
107 `net_accept` failures, all -126 (`ENETRESET`), 91 of them in the same
millisecond as a gateway probe that had failed with -126, about 1.2 s after
the network came up. `loader.c` took the accept's `ENETRESET` as a network
reset; `net_init` then stayed busy for 12.7 s, failed with -116, and HBC
relaunched with a fresh IOS.

- `netprobe.c`: the first keep-alive and probe wait `HBC_NETPROBE_SETTLE_MS`
  (5 s) after the network comes up. On the first boot with it, the probe
  answered in 98 ms at +8.6 s; before, the probe at +3.6 s took 1,019 ms
  when it answered at all.
- `loader.c`: a lone `ENETRESET` from `net_accept` keeps the listener and is
  logged; `ACCEPT_RESETS` (3) in a row restart the network as before.

**This does not fix the drop.** After `slicks-test` returned, with no probe
sent, `net_accept` got -126 at +4661, +4678 and +4694 ms, the network reset,
`net_init` failed with -116 and HBC relaunched. IOS's stack resets about
1.2 s after HBC's `net_init` following an app return; the probe was a second
victim of that, not its cause. Open: slicks exited with the network still
up (no `net_deinit` before its IOS reload), which HBC's own exits never do;
its next build closes the network first, to see whether the reset goes.

**libogc 3.2.1 breaks the Wii Remotes in HBC.** Built on
`devkitpro/devkitppc` of 2026-11-02 (libogc 3.2.1), HBC took no Wii Remote:
its lights blinked and went out, and status listed none. The same remote
connected in the System Menu, and the same source built on libogc 3.1.0
took it at once. The cause is HBC's own `wpadqueue.c`: it wraps
`wiiuse_init_cmd_queue` (`-Wl,--wrap`) to give libogc 3.1's queues heap
memory, as `void`. libogc 3.2.0 made that function return `int`, and
`wiiuse_init` fails on a non-zero return, which the wrapper leaves as junk,
so `WPAD_Init` fails. libogc 3.2 also allocates the queues with `malloc`
itself, so on 3.2 the wrapper should go, not be ported. Not a libogc bug.
`wpadqueue.c` and `fpurelease.h` now both stop a build on any libogc but
3.1. (`fpu_release` was checked against the 3.2.1 source and still holds
there: SPRG3 is still the FPU owner, and `KThreadExit` still does not clear
it.)

Built on libogc 3.1.0 and installed on Matt's Wii with `tests/apps/wadinstall`
on IOS 249; the Wii Remote connects. `tests/host/test_netprobe.py` checks that
nothing is sent during the settle time.

## 1.16.1: the Updated badge clears, the call log fits, the agent shrinks

Also in 1.16.1:

- Updated badge: an amber light inside the button's top edge that fades
  downward, following the button's outline, instead of a 3 px line.
- Start-up call log: a fast poll that repeats takes one row with a count
  (`repeats`, `last_out`), so the drive scan no longer fills the log.
- hbc_agent: the overlay's and the Log page's big buffers are allocated only
  while used (overlay static memory 45.7 KB → 4.7 KB, agent 29.2 KB → 8.6 KB).
- HBCS adds `uptime_ms`, `net_longest` and `accepts`; `hbc.py send` sees a
  new HBC by its uptime. `tcp_close` waits in 10 ms slices (longest network
  call 3.6 s → 0.5 s on a Wii); `hbc.py` polls HBCS less hard.
- Tests: `ensure_hbc` skips re-sending the running build; Dolphin tests poll
  instead of sleeping and run together with `tests/dolphin/run_all.py`; new
  `tests/wii/wii_app_badges.py` and `tests/wii/wii_port_outage.py`; lrwii
  is a bench Wii (only prod is refused).

Checked on the dev Wii, 2026-11-10: `wii_app_badges` ALL PASS (new 0, after a
run 0, changed boot.dol 482-486 amber pixels focused or not, identical send
clears it, sort_id-only change 0). All 22 Dolphin tests pass. Open: IOS's
TCP stack sometimes stops answering port 4299 for about 2.5 minutes (open
item in [review.md](../review.md), [research/ios-tcp.md](../research/ios-tcp.md)).

### Two causes of the Updated badge staying on

- Trigger (dev Wii, 2026-11-09): `hbc.py order` rewrites `meta.xml` to set
  `<sort_id>`, so every reordered app turned Updated; and an app installed in
  `sd:/apps/X` and launched by `hbc.py send`/`run` was never recorded, so it
  stayed Updated for good.
- Fix: `apps_seen.txt` stores `meta.xml` as an FNV-1a hash of its text without
  `<sort_id>...</sort_id>` and without white space around tags (`meta.xml=h:HEX8`).
  An old `meta.xml=SIZE@TIME` stamp still parses and is judged by size and time
  until the app's next run (which rewrites it, even when nothing changed). A network
  launch whose HBCA caption is `<app>/boot.dol|elf` records `sd:/apps/<app>` when
  the bytes sent equal that folder's file (size, then content; checked before the
  loader touches the data). Other uploads record nothing.
- Check: `tests/host/test_apprun.py` (sort_id-only change, real edit, old-format line,
  upload match and its refusals); `tests/dolphin/dolphin_app_badges.py` has a
  new `<sort_id>` step; on the dev Wii, `tests/wii/wii_app_badges.py` confirmed that
  `hbc.py send sd:/apps/X/boot.dol` clears X's Updated pill.

## 1.16.0: release; a stuck USB drive no longer holds HBC on a black screen

The release of 1.15.1 to 1.15.21 (details in [1.15.md](1.15.md)). The headline:

- A reset with a USB drive mid-transfer used to leave HBC on a black screen
  until the drive was pulled. HBC now finds the stuck drive at start-up,
  frees IOS with a software unplug, and starts with its menu up; until the
  drive is pulled, a menu launch warns first and a network launch is refused.
- [sdk/usbguard](../../sdk/usbguard/README.md) gives other apps the same
  protection: guarded USB calls, mounts and IOS reloads, a warning screen, the
  software unplug and the wait for a replug. Public domain.
- `hbc.py status` lists HBC's timed start-up calls, and the TV shows them when
  the menu is late.

Also since 1.15.0: the audit fixes of 1.15.2-1.15.12, the slot light library
fixes (1.15.13-1.15.15), `hbc.py send --no-wait`, a pulled drive leaving status,
and a file that is not a DOL being refused instead of crashing HBC. The old
forwarding headers `sdk/hbc_agent.h` and `sdk/hbc_netlog.h` are gone; use
`-I<hbc-reborn>/sdk/hbc_agent` and `-I<hbc-reborn>/sdk/hbc_netlog`.

For contributors: the repository is reorganised (tests by where they run, docs
in `docs/`, every SDK in its own folder), and `tools/hooks/pre-push`
(`git config core.hooksPath tools/hooks`) stops a push that carries Wii keys,
signing keys, dumps or tokens.

Checked on the dev Wii, 2026-11-09, HBC 1.15.21 installed as OHBC, with Monty
at the Wii:

- Stuck USB (`tests/wii/wii_usbguard.py`, d2x usb-spin, job
  20261109-094510-6f5895): ALL PASS. After each of two watchdog resets HBC
  freed the stuck drive and its menu was up at about 1.6 s. Round 1: HBC's
  launch warning, the app's warning at 5.0 s, the pull seen, and a mount after
  the replug. Round 2: the software unplug freed the stuck call (FREED).
- Replug (`tests/wii/wii_replug.py`, job 20261109-095750-d23029): 6 of 8 steps
  passed. Known issue: after both devices were out, an SD card plugged in
  after the USB drive is not found until HBC restarts (open item "The SD card
  is not found again after both devices were out").
